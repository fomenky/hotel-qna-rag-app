🏨 AuroraStay QnA — RAG Intelligence Report

Deploying GenAI Solutions • Policy Question-Answering POC for hotel staff

Docling parsing HarishMaths/Hotel-Policy-Embedding FAISS + BM25 hybrid gpt-4o-mini DeepEval (gpt-4o judge) Flask + Streamlit on Codespaces

📋 Business Objective

AuroraStay Hotels handles ~1,200 guest queries/week; ~40% are complex, spanning multiple policy documents and taking 8–12 minutes each to answer manually with a 15–20% error rate. The goal of this POC is an internal-facing assistant that answers natural-language policy questions grounded only in the hotel's documents, shows its sources, and reduces the share of queries that require manual lookup or escalation — validated for accuracy and reliability before any broader rollout.

🔧 System Architecture

  1. Ingestion: four policy PDFs parsed with Docling into structured Markdown (tables preserved).
  2. Chunking: a table-aware key:value splitter renders every table row as a labelled sentence (Section — Row. Column: value; …) so field names sit inline with values; prose is split token-aware under the embedding model's 128-token limit. Boilerplate is dropped. → 144 chunks.
  3. Retrieval: hybrid — FAISS semantic search (the prescribed fine-tuned embedding model) + BM25 keyword search, merged with reciprocal-rank fusion (k=8).
  4. Generation: gpt-4o-mini (temperature 0) with answer-forcing rules that ground the response in retrieved text, map guest wording to property codes, and decline when information is absent.
  5. Evaluation: DeepEval GEval metrics judged by gpt-4o (a stronger, independent model).
  6. Deployment: Flask API (3 endpoints) + Streamlit UI, containerised and run on GitHub Codespaces.

📈 Actionable Insights

1. Most routine queries are handled without manual lookup

90% of test-set and 60% of evaluation-set queries cleared the AUTO-ANSWER bar — both above the ≥50% business target.

2. Answers are grounded and on-topic

High Faithfulness means the assistant answers from retrieved policy text and does not invent fees, deadlines, or conditions; when information is genuinely absent it declines rather than guessing.

3. The remaining gap is retrieval, not generation

Contextual Precision (and eval-set Recall) trail the thresholds, driven by a few out-of-corpus questions (a property not in the documents) and vocabulary-mismatched / multi-entity questions (“how many people” vs “occupancy”; “loud” vs “quiet hours”; two properties in one question) that the fine-tuned embedder does not always rank correctly.

📝 Recommendations

  1. Deploy as a human-in-the-loop assistant. Route AUTO-ANSWER results to staff directly; send ASSISTED-REVIEW / MANUAL-LOOKUP items to the existing process. Captures the gain on the answerable majority while protecting against the low-confidence minority.
  2. Invest in retrieval next, not generation. Faithfulness and relevancy already pass; target recall/precision via light query normalization for domain synonyms, a reranking/stronger retriever, and broader document coverage.
  3. Keep “not enough information” as a feature. Correct, safe declining paired with a clear escalation path.
  4. Monitor with the same four metrics in production, tracking rolling averages and the AUTO-ANSWER rate, and re-baseline whenever documents or properties change.
